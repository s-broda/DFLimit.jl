Two engines

The package holds two engines. The classical engine, DFLimit.DFExact (src/DFExact.jl), is that of the companion paper: the line and conditional integral representations for the three classical cases and case DG, and for a fixed start in cases DU, DT the contour integral (the inversion on paths of the lead paper, the integral over v in closed form). The general engine, DFLimit.DFDet (src/DFDet.jl, the file of the repository df-deterministics, with its outer paths of the classical cases at the unit root in data/paths), is that of the lead paper: it computes the same limits for any deterministic terms (piecewise polynomial or smooth functions of r = t/n with known break dates, the statistic that detrends first, GLS detrending) by the inversion formula on paths around the singularities of the joint moment generating function. They stay two submodules, each its own file as at its home (the statistics of the lead paper beyond the t-ratio are a file of their own, src/DFDet_paper2.jl, evaluated in the module DFDet): the engines were written separately and share many internal names, and kept apart each can be brought up to date by copying its file. The one file that knows both is src/merged.jl: the interface, the router that the classical user-level functions consult after their own checks of the arguments (DFExact.ROUTE), and the rule DFLimit.autoengine.

One interface

callcomputes
ltratio_cdf(x, D, c = 0; c0, g0, start, gls, method, engine), ltratio_pdfthe t-ratio of any design D
ltratio_icdf(p, D, c = 0; ...), ltratio_mom(p, D, c = 0; ...)its quantiles and moments
coef_cdf(y, D, c = 0; ...), coef_icdf(p, D, c = 0; ...)the coefficient statistic
adfmax_cdf(x, D, c = 0; start), adfmax_pdf, adfmax_icdfADFmax
ltratio_lincdf(b, a, D, c = 0; gls)P(U - aV <= b) (a = NaN: P(V <= b))
ltratio_region(bnd, D, c = 0)P{U <= g(V)}: a line or the parabola of the t-ratio
power_envelope(c, D; alpha)the Gaussian power envelope of the design

The design D is trend(p) (p = -1: no deterministic terms, 0: an intercept, 1: a linear trend, and any p), perron(:A, lambda), perron(:B, lambda), perron(:C, lambda) (lambda a break date or a vector of them), fdesign(f, breaks) (regressors given by a function), ltrend(p) (an orthonormal polynomial trend), design_from_matrix(X) (from the regressor matrix of a regression) or detrend_first(D) (the statistic that detrends first); the start is g0 (a fixed start J(0) = g0) or start = :stationary, and gls = cbar GLS-detrends at abar = 1 + cbar/n. A design that is a classical case is that case: ltratio_cdf(x, trend(0), c) is ltratio_cdf(x, "DU", c), trend(1) with start = :stationary is case ST and with gls = -13.5 case DG. The case strings keep the classical engine's checks of the arguments (the range of c, the region of the fixed start), as in 0.5.0; a design beyond them goes to the general engine. Every call takes the engine of autoengine (engine = :auto); engine = :classical forces the classical engine (an ArgumentError for a design it does not have) and engine = :general the general one (without the classical checks; method then names the general engine's representation, :auto, :line or :paths). A method other than :auto, verified = true and the classical engine's own keywords keep the classical engine for a case string. Where the general engine computes (a design the classical engine does not have, or engine = :general), method is the general engine's own: :auto, :line (the line integral with the explicit kernel) or :paths (the inversion on paths around the roots), see Which engine; for a design that is a classical case method = :line is the classical line integral and method = :paths the general engine's paths, so that ltratio_cdf(x, trend(2), c; method = :paths) and ltratio_icdf(p, trend(0), c; method = :paths) force the paths and ltratio_cdf(x, "DU", c; engine = :general, method = :line) the general engine's line integral. The general engine is checked from c = -480 to 10, and above the unit root, for the coefficient statistic, the moments and the t-ratio centred at the true value (c0 = c), up to c = 16 with deterministic terms (10 with them and a fixed start) and up to c = 20 without them (DFLimit.DFDet.gen_cmax): beyond, one root of its transform is of the order 2c e^{-2c} and the others are no longer resolved in double precision, and every function of the general engine raises an ArgumentError that names the statistic, c and the bound; the rule takes the classical engine there. Far above the unit root, for c > 5.5, the inversion on paths by one evaluation may be off by more than 1e-5 in the upper tail for a design beyond the classical cases and for a fixed start with deterministic terms (Perron's model C at c = 7 by 1.5e-5 in F, a linear trend with a fixed start at c = 7 by 8.7e-5), and the general engine warns there (verified = true checks it). Each of the general engine's warnings of a brittle computation (this one, the vertical line's parts at a root of the parabola, the rounding estimate and the end of the outer contour) comes at most once per call, also where the call evaluates many times (the steps of a quantile search, the quantiles of a vector of probabilities, the normal average of rho; DFDet.once_per_call).

The general engine's own functions are exported as well: dist_cdf(D, xs, c; c0, start, gamma0, gls, method), dist_pdf, dist_quantile, dist_moments, coef_pdf, coef_quantile, coef_cdf_gp, lin_cdf, lin_pdf, lin_quantile, v_cdf, v_pdf, adfmax_quantile, region_prob, local_power, simulate_tratio, simulate_gls, simulate_first and the designs; its coef_cdf, adfmax_cdf, adfmax_pdf and power_envelope, whose names are the classical functions', are DFLimit.DFDet.coef_cdf and so on. The statistics of the lead paper beyond the t-ratio are functions of the general engine: the point-optimal statistic of Elliott, Rothenberg and Stock and the power envelope for any design (pt_cdf(D, qs, cbar, c), pt_pdf, pt_quantile(D, p, cbar), pt_power(D, cbar, c), pt_envelope(D, cs), also through power_envelope(c, D); for the intercept and the linear trend it is the envelope of the classical construction to 1e-8), the LM statistic of Schmidt and Phillips and of Amsler and Lee (lm_cdf(D, xs, c), lm_quantile(D, p), lm_power(D, cs); 5% value -3.0230 for the linear trend; for trend functions whose span contains the constant and a linear trend, an ArgumentError otherwise, DFDet.span_has_constant) and the companions of the GLS-detrended t-ratio, MZalpha (`mzacdf(D, ys, c; gls),mzaquantile) and MSB (msbcdf(D, xs, c; gls),msb_quantile`).
