Seasonal unit roots

At a frequency strictly between 0 and pi (pi/2 for quarterly data; each pair of harmonic frequencies of monthly data) a pair of complex unit roots gives the regression of Hylleberg, Engle, Granger and Yoo two t-ratios and an F statistic. Their limit process is complex: Z = Wa + i Wb with independent standard Brownian motions, dZ = c Z dr + dW, Z(0) = 0 (c in the units of the roots' modulus 1 + c/n, n the number of years), and Z~ its residual on the deterministic terms. With

R = Re int Z~* dZ,   V = int |Z~|^2

the first t-ratio is t3 = (R - c0 V)/sqrt(V), the t-ratio of pi3 in the quarterly numbering (the coefficient of y{3,t-2}), of Dickey-Fuller shape. The package computes t3; the second t-ratio (of pi_4) and the F statistic of the same regression are not part of this release. The deterministic terms are the real trend functions of a design of the general engine, entering with complex coefficients: trend(-1) for a regression without seasonal terms (a constant or a linear trend leave the harmonic statistics alone), trend(0) for seasonal dummies (with or without a trend), trend(1) for seasonal dummies and seasonal trends, and any other design (breaks in the seasonal means, ...). At the frequencies 0 and pi the roots are real and the statistics are the Dickey-Fuller t-ratios of the classical functions (with seasonal dummies the intercept's).

The computation

(R, V) is the sum of two independent copies of the lead paper's (U, V), so its transform is e^{-s}/D(s, t) with the general engine's determinant D, whose singularities in s are poles: the inner integral of the inversion on paths is the sum of the residues at the roots, and the density and the distribution function are single integrals over the engine's outer contour of closed-form functions (the engine's kernels at the roots). This holds for every design of the engine and for a volatility path (vol). Where two roots of a group come within 1e-4 (relative) of each other along the contour, the residues are replaced by the engine's inversion on paths of the same transform with every root doubled.

Calls

callcomputes
hegy_t3_cdf(D, xs, c = 0; c0, vol), hegy_t3_pdf, hegy_t3_quantile(D, p, c; x0, tol, c0, vol)t3, any design, a volatility path

They take the zero start and c <= 0. verified = true gives t3 by both computations (a warning where they differ by more than vtol).

Examples

julia> hegy_t3_quantile(trend(0), [0.01, 0.05, 0.1])        # t3 with seasonal dummies
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 -3.9088938821313146
 -3.3442470388202103
 -3.0477235222336314

julia> hegy_t3_cdf(trend(0), [-3.3442], -5.0)               # the local power of the 5% t3 test at c = -5
1-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.20176667884727695

julia> hegy_t3_cdf(trend(0), [-3.3442]; vol = volpath([0.5], [1.0, 3.0]))   # its size under a variance break
1-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.027813961380253382

Accuracy and cost

The residues agree with the engine's inversion on paths of the doubled transform to 2.4e-13 (designs with and without deterministic terms, with breaks, c from 0 to -20 and -50, c0 of either sign), with the exponential law of |Z(1)|^2/2 without deterministic terms (P(t3 <= 0) = 1 - 1/e) to 2e-16, and with the elementary form at the unit root in spherical Bessel functions to 1e-12; the moments of the density of t3 agree with Nabeya's (2001) table to its eight decimals, and the critical values and powers of the prototype's table (checked there against simulations of the regression) are reproduced to their four.

Times on warm calls (four threads of an M4 laptop on mains power with nothing else running, November 26, 2026): t3 0.04 to 0.06 ms a value on a cached contour and its 5% quantile in 0.2 to 0.5 ms.
